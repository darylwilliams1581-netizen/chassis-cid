# Year 9 maths — sample notes

These notes are a short original summary written for testing school mode.
They are not copied from any published textbook.

## Pythagoras

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. If those sides are a and b, and the hypotenuse is c, then a squared plus b squared equals c squared.

A right-angled triangle has one angle of 90 degrees. The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle, and it is the longest side.

## Order of operations

Work out brackets first, then indices (powers), then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. A common reminder for this order is BIDMAS or BODMAS.

Example: 3 plus 4 times 2 equals 11, not 14, because multiplication is done before addition.

## Directed numbers

Adding a negative number is the same as subtracting the corresponding positive number. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the corresponding positive number. The product of two negative numbers is positive.
